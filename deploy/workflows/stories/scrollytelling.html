Scrollytelling

This page loads scrollama from a CDN and drives a sticky graphic with it. If you are reading this inside a workspace panel, it is running in a sandboxed iframe — the question this page answers is whether that changes anything.

It should not. scrollama is a thin wrapper over IntersectionObserver, and an observer created inside a frame uses that frame's own viewport as its root. The story scrolls inside its panel, the observer watches that scrolling, and neither needs to know the frame is embedded.

Why it works in a panel

The frame is its own scrolling document. IntersectionObserver created inside it takes that frame's viewport as its root, so it sees the story scroll exactly as it would in a full browser window. position: sticky works for the same reason.

None of this needs to reach out of the frame, which matters: the story runs on an opaque origin and cannot read the workspace around it.

The one case where it breaks

A panel declared fit="content" grows to the height of its content, so the frame becomes as tall as the whole story and never scrolls internally. The workspace scrolls instead — and the observer inside the frame cannot see that, because it is watching a viewport that is no longer moving. Every step is intersecting at once, and nothing fires progressively.

So: keep a scrollytelling story on the default fit="scroll".

workspace.Story("stories/scrollytelling.xmd")               # correct
workspace.Story("stories/scrollytelling.xmd", fit="content")  # breaks it

That is a real constraint rather than a bug to fix: the two behaviours are mutually exclusive. A frame either scrolls itself or is sized to its content, and scrollytelling needs the first.
